Automatic upgrade rule

Two calmness scores of 4 or 5 on the same challenge stage move Milo up one stage. A score of 3 repeats the stage. Two scores of 1 or 2 on that stage move him down.

Targets of 15 minutes or longer are future-stage work. Do only one full long challenge attempt in a day; the other practices should be short, easy consolidation sessions. Milo is still very young, so never rush the ladder.

How Progression Works

Upgrade after two calm results

  • Use the same challenge stage for both attempts.
  • Enter a score of 4 or 5 for each attempt.
  • The website then recommends the next stage.
  • Long-stage attempts may happen on different days.

Repeat or move down

  • Score 3: repeat the current challenge stage.
  • Two scores of 1 or 2: move down one stage.
  • Stop early if crying becomes sharper or continuous.
  • Check toileting, tiredness and comfort first.

Long stages are future goals

The 30-minute, 45-minute and one-hour plans are not immediate targets for a six-week-old puppy. They are included so the tracker can grow with Milo over time.

Extended guide information

Extra Training Information

Preparation, crying adjustments, scoring and long-stage rules.

Important for Milo's age

Milo is still extremely young. The advanced 30-minute-to-one-hour ladder is a future roadmap, not a schedule to rush through now. His bladder control, sleep needs and ability to self-settle are still developing.

Before every session

  • Offer a toilet break first.
  • Train when he is awake and reasonably calm, not overtired or extremely hungry.
  • Use part of his normal kibble allowance.
  • Provide one safe familiar toy or chew.
  • Keep departures and returns ordinary.

What to do when he cries

What you seeWhat to do
Brief whine or one or two barksWait for a tiny pause, reward calmness and make the next repetition easy.
Repeated barking but he still pauses and eatsReduce the planned absence by about half and add a visible recovery period.
Continuous sharper crying, frantic scratching or refusing foodReturn calmly, help him settle and end or move to a much easier stage.
Sudden crying after previously copingCheck toileting, tiredness, temperature and physical comfort.

Rewarding after barking

It is acceptable to reward the first one-second pause after barking. You are rewarding the quiet pause. Make the next absence shorter so calmness can be rewarded before barking begins.

Calmness scale

  1. 1: escalating and unable to settle.
  2. 2: frequently distressed.
  3. 3: mixed but manageable.
  4. 4: mostly calm and recovers well.
  5. 5: calm and confident.

Two 4-or-5 results on the same challenge stage upgrade Milo. Two 1-or-2 results move him down.

Rules for 15 minutes and longer

  • Do only one full long challenge attempt in a day.
  • Use an audio or video monitor when possible.
  • Do not complete the timer while distress escalates.
  • Other daily practices should be short and easy.
  • Toileting and age-appropriate alone-time limits still matter.
