How the automatic recommendation works

Two scores of 4 or 5 in a row on the same session move Milo to the next harder session. Two scores of 1 or 2 in a row move him to an easier session. Any other score pattern repeats the current session.

Three sessions are available as a daily maximum. Skip or shorten a session when Milo is overtired, unwell, unable to take food or becoming more distressed.

When Should Milo Move Up?

Move up one session when:

  • He scores 4 or 5 twice in a row.
  • Both scores are for the same length and level.
  • He can still take treats.
  • His crying stays brief and manageable.

Repeat or move down when:

  • Two scores of 1 or 2 occur in a row.
  • His crying becomes more intense.
  • He cannot settle even when you return.
  • He scratches frantically or refuses food.

Helpful reminder

A small amount of crying does not mean the session failed. Record what happened and follow the recommendation shown for the next session.

How the plan works

The plan combines two methods: staying nearby while calm and boring, and gradually adding movement, disappearances and returns. Harder does not mean allowing more distress. It means Milo can manage slightly more distance, movement or time out of sight.

Important for Milo's age

Milo is exceptionally young. Keep sessions gentle, use many easy repetitions and stop before he becomes overtired.

Before every session
  • Give Milo a toilet opportunity first.
  • Train when he is awake and reasonably calm, not extremely hungry, overexcited or overtired.
  • Use part of his normal daily kibble allowance rather than unlimited extra treats.
  • Place one safe toy or chew in the pen and supervise anything that could be chewed apart.
  • Keep the room calm so the session feels ordinary, not like a test.

The PDF suggests starting with one or two sessions per day. This tracker allows up to three, but the third is optional when Milo needs more rest.

What to do when Milo cries
What you see What it may mean What to do
Brief whine or one or two barks Mild protest or frustration Wait for a tiny pause, reward calmness and continue with an easier repetition.
Repeated barking, but he still eats and pauses The level may be slightly difficult Reduce the distance or absence by about half and add an easy recovery minute.
Continuous sharper crying, frantic scratching or inability to eat The session is too difficult Return calmly, help him settle and end or drop to a much easier level.
Sudden crying after previously coping He may need the toilet, be tired or feel uncomfortable Check practical needs before assuming it is a training problem.

Rewarding after barking

It is acceptable to reward the first one-second pause after barking. The next repetition should then be easier, so calmness can be rewarded before barking starts.

Do not turn it into a battle

Never shout, tap the pen, stare Milo down or deliberately leave him to escalate. The goal is confidence, not proving that he can be ignored.

When to move up, repeat or move down
  • Repeat a level until Milo completes it successfully at least twice on different attempts.
  • Move up when most of the session is quiet or only mildly vocal, he takes food and settles after small challenges.
  • Move down when crying grows, he cannot eat or each repetition becomes harder.
  • Levels can be mixed. A harder morning session can be followed by an easier session later.

In this website, the automatic rule uses two consecutive scores of 4–5 to move up and two consecutive scores of 1–2 to move down.

A good session is not necessarily silent

A good session is one where Milo remains safe, can recover from small protests and finishes at the same or a lower stress level than he started.

PDF calm score guide

The original PDF uses a separate 0–3 stress-style guide. Your website keeps its existing 1–5 calmness score for recommendations.

  1. Relaxed, playing, sniffing or lying down; little or no crying.
  2. Alert with brief whining, but settles easily and takes food.
  3. Repeated barking or whining, but still pauses and can recover.
  4. Escalating or continuous distress, frantic behaviour or refusal of food.
Final reminders
  • Milo does not need to complete every level quickly. Confidence is built through repetition.
  • Increase only one main difficulty at a time: distance, movement, time out of sight or total session length.
  • Always include easier minutes after a difficult repetition.
  • A one-second quiet pause is enough to reward at the beginning.
  • Check toileting, tiredness, hunger, temperature and comfort when behaviour suddenly worsens.
  • Use the pen for pleasant everyday experiences such as meals, toys and naps, not only training.
  • Seek guidance from a veterinarian or qualified force-free trainer if crying becomes intense, Milo cannot settle or his behaviour changes suddenly.

The overall rule

Make the next repetition easy enough that Milo has a realistic chance to succeed. Many manageable repetitions build confidence better than one session that is too hard.
